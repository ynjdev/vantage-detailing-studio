What It Actually Is

Correction Removes Defects.
It Doesn't Hide Them.

Most "polishes" fill swirl marks with oils and wax. They look good for a fortnight, then the fillers wash out and the defects come straight back.

Machine correction removes a measured amount of clear coat, levelling the surface so the defect no longer exists. It's permanent — but it's also finite. Your paint only has so much clear coat, which is why we measure depth before touching anything.

That measurement is the difference between a detailer who corrects your paint and one who gambles with it.

FAQ

Paint Correction Questions

Will correction remove every scratch?

No, and anyone who says yes is guessing. If a scratch catches your fingernail it's usually through the clear coat — correcting it risks burning through. We measure the paint, tell you what's realistically removable, and leave the rest alone rather than damage the panel.

Is machine polishing safe for my paint?

When it's measured, yes. We take paint depth readings before starting and work to the softest pad and least aggressive compound that achieves the result. The risk comes from detailers who skip the measurement.

How long does the correction last?

The correction itself is permanent — those defects are gone. What matters is preventing new ones, which comes down to wash technique and whether the paint is protected afterwards. Coating over corrected paint is what makes the work last.

Do I need coating after correction?

Not compulsory, but it's the logical follow-on. You've just paid to remove defects — a coating makes it much harder for new ones to form and makes washing safer. We apply a sealant as standard either way.

Can you correct at my office?

Yes, as long as there's access to water and a power point near the car, and enough space to work around it. Covered parking is ideal but not required.
